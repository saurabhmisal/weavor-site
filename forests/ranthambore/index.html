The landscape & its story

Get to know Ranthambore.

Ranthambore brings together dry woodland, lakes and a historic fort landscape. Dhok trees cover the slopes, deer pause near water and birds use both the forest and lakeshores. The setting feels different from the sal forests of Central India and fits naturally into a wider journey through Rajasthan.

Tigers are an important part of this protected landscape, alongside leopards, sloth bears and crocodiles. Watching how animals use water, shade and rocky ground gives each drive a purpose beyond searching for one species. Your naturalist helps connect those observations with the place’s ecology and history.

Planning your visit

Make room for the journey.

When to travel

The principal safari tourism season is generally October to June, but zone access and monsoon arrangements can vary. Confirm your exact permit dates and eligible zones before making other arrangements.

Getting there

Sawai Madhopur is the rail gateway. Jaipur is a common road connection; allow around 3 hours to the main lodge area, with the final timing confirmed for your route.

How long to stay

Consider 2–4 nights as a starting point, leaving time for several drives and rest between them. We adapt this to your interests and the rest of your India journey.

Transfer times are approximate. Your final route depends on traffic, the entry gate and accommodation; current access and permits are checked for your travel dates.

Questions about Ranthambore.

Can Ranthambore be part of a Rajasthan journey?

Yes. It can be combined with time in Jaipur, Delhi or other cultural destinations depending on the route and time available.

Is the park suitable for birdwatchers?

Yes. Its lakes and woodland provide opportunities to see waterbirds, peafowl, raptors and other forest birds.
