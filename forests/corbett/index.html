The landscape & its story

Get to know Corbett.

Corbett sits in the Himalayan foothills, where sal woodland, grassland and the Ramganga River create a different setting from Central India’s forests. You may spend a drive looking for elephant signs, watching birds along the water or learning how wildlife moves between the open ground and tree cover.

The diversity of habitats is central to the reserve’s value. Tigers belong to this larger landscape alongside deer, birds and river life. Different tourism zones have their own access arrangements, so we build your visit around the season, available permits and the part of the forest you will explore.

Planning your visit

Make room for the journey.

When to travel

Opening dates vary by safari zone. The reserve lists Dhikala reopening on 15 November and Dhela as a year-round tourism zone, subject to current conditions. Confirm the zone and dates before travel.

Getting there

Delhi → the Ramnagar/Dhikuli area: allow roughly 5–7 hours by road, depending on traffic and your stay. Rail may suit some itineraries.

How long to stay

Consider 3–4 nights as a starting point, leaving time for several drives and rest between them. We adapt this to your interests and the rest of your India journey.

Transfer times are approximate. Your final route depends on traffic, the entry gate and accommodation; current access and permits are checked for your travel dates.

About this destination

Questions about Corbett.

Can I see elephants in Corbett?

Wild Asian elephants live in the wider reserve and may be encountered in permitted tourism areas. Like all wildlife sightings, encounters are not guaranteed.

Are all safari zones open at the same time?

No. Seasonal schedules vary by zone, and some close earlier than others. We check the specific areas appropriate for your travel dates.
