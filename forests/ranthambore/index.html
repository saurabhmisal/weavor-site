Get to know Ranthambore

See the forest through local eyes.

Few places combine wilderness and history quite like Ranthambore. The forest unfolds beneath old stone walls, past lakes and dry wooded slopes. A sambar may pause beside the water. Peafowl call from the trees. Somewhere in this rocky landscape, a tiger moves through a place with a much longer story.

Ranthambore's striking scenery comes from its position between the Aravalli and Vindhya ranges. It is known for Bengal tigers, but leopards, sloth bears, crocodiles, deer and a wide range of dry-forest and waterbirds also make this a richly layered journey.

A memorable pairing of wildlife, landscapes and Rajasthan's history.

Our journeys are about learning to notice the forest, not chasing wildlife for a photograph. There's always more to discover, even when a tiger stays out of sight.

Understand the landscape

The wild among old stone walls

Ranthambore is a good reminder that forests have both ecological and cultural histories. The old fort, ruins, lakes and dry woodland share one landscape. Seeing them together gives the park a character unlike Central India's sal forests and meadows.

Plan with confidence

What to know before you go.

We'll help you plan around park regulations, permit availability and sensible travel connections. The right pace matters more than collecting as many drives as possible.

01

When to travel

The principal safari tourism season is generally October to June, but zone access and monsoon arrangements can vary. Confirm your exact permit dates and eligible zones before making other arrangements.

02

Getting there

Sawai Madhopur is the main rail gateway. Many international visitors combine Ranthambore with Jaipur, Delhi or Agra by road and rail, depending on their itinerary.

03

How long to stay

We suggest starting with 2 to 4 nights. We'll work out an itinerary that suits your pace, preferred accommodation and other places you hope to visit in India.

Safari permits, entry zones, vehicle arrangements, access restrictions and seasons are determined by the responsible forest authorities. We confirm current rules before you book.

Before you travel

Things you may be wondering.

What makes Ranthambore different from other tiger reserves?

Its dry woodland, lakes, rugged hills and historic fort landscape create a combination of wildlife and history that feels quite different from Central India.

Can Ranthambore be part of a Rajasthan journey?

Yes. It can be combined with time in Jaipur, Delhi or other cultural destinations depending on the route and time available.

Are tiger sightings guaranteed here?

No. Ranthambore is well known for tigers, but every safari follows the movement of wild animals and the rules of the reserve.

Is the park suitable for birdwatchers?

Yes. Its lakes and woodland provide opportunities to see waterbirds, peafowl, raptors and other forest birds.

Will I definitely see a tiger?

No. Tigers and other animals are wild, and no sighting is guaranteed. We suggest allowing several drives and staying curious about everything the forest reveals.
