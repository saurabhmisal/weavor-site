The landscape & its story

Get to know Kanha.

Kanha’s open meadows invite you to slow down and watch. Deer graze against a backdrop of sal woodland, birds move along the forest edge and changes in light reveal activity you might otherwise miss. This is a rewarding place to learn how grassland and woodland support different animals.

The hard-ground barasingha, a distinctive swamp deer, is at the heart of Kanha’s conservation story. Its recovery helps explain why protecting habitat matters alongside protecting individual species. Your naturalist can put that story in context while introducing the wider forest, from grazing herds to birds and smaller discoveries.

Planning your visit

Make room for the journey.

When to travel

Tourism in the core zones usually runs from mid-October until the end of June. Exact calendars, safari days and any buffer-zone access should be confirmed for the season you plan to visit.

Getting there

Jabalpur → the Khatia-side lodge area: around 3 hours by road. Kanha’s other gates may need a different route and more travel time.

How long to stay

Consider 3–4 nights as a starting point, leaving time for several drives and rest between them. We adapt this to your interests and the rest of your India journey.

Transfer times are approximate. Your final route depends on traffic, the entry gate and accommodation; current access and permits are checked for your travel dates.

About this forest

Questions about Kanha.

Why is Kanha famous for barasingha?

Kanha is central to the survival of the hard-ground barasingha. Conservation and habitat work have helped the species recover.

Is Kanha suitable for people interested in birds and plants?

Yes. Its mosaic of woodland and grasslands supports rich birdlife and makes the forest itself part of the experience.

Can Kanha and Bandhavgarh be combined?

Yes. They make a natural Central India pairing for a longer trip, provided you allow enough time for the road transfer and multiple safari drives.
